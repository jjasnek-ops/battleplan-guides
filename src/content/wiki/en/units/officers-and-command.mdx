## How does an officer affect the command chain?

An officer's effects apply to troops beneath that officer in the hierarchy, according to the official Officers diary. This makes assignment and organization important together: read which troops are subordinate to the headquarters before assuming a skill benefits the entire army. Some skills apply broadly; others affect particular unit types.

The [Army Constructor guide](/units/army-constructor/) explains how to inspect and change the connections. This guide describes the documented command relationships without a launch-build stacking formula or numerical skill ranking. The February 2026 diary predates release, so current tooltip descriptions take precedence for values, restrictions and changes.

## What is the difference between passive and active skills?

Passive skills give ongoing effects to eligible troops under the officer's command. Active skills need manual activation within the mission and may require the headquarters company to move to a designated area. Read the current ability's range, duration and use restrictions before ordering a vulnerable headquarters forward for a temporary benefit.

| Skill type | Use decision | Risk to check |
|---|---|---|
| Passive | Match the effect to eligible subordinate units | A specialist effect may not help every unit in a mixed formation. |
| Active | Choose the moment and location deliberately | The HQ's required movement may expose the officer to enemy fire. |
| Personality quirk | Weigh the benefit and penalty together | A useful offensive trait may carry an unwanted cost for a holding force. |

The diary says many active skills have limited use per mission. It does not justify assuming every current skill has the same charge count. This page deliberately omits old percentage examples.

## How should you choose an officer for a formation?

Start with the formation's role and inspect both its eligible skill effects and personality costs. An officer supporting a mobile attack serves a different task from one commanding infantry that must hold ground. Check the actual troops beneath the HQ and their assignment before choosing by level or portrait alone.

1. State the formation's current task.
2. Compare that task with the officer's skill descriptions.
3. Read each quirk's benefit and penalty.
4. Check which subordinate units can use the effect.
5. Reassess after restructuring or changing the formation's task.

Use the [unit reference](/units/unit-types/) to identify the force's roles and the [combat guide](/guides/combat-guide/) to decide what support the operation needs. These are selection principles, not an untested list of “best officers.”

## Why should headquarters stay out of frontline fighting?

Headquarters companies are command elements with reduced combat strength, not replacements for frontline infantry. The officer diary describes them trying to disengage when attacked. Casualties can wound, capture or kill the officer, and incapacitation during a mission can temporarily disrupt the entire formation. Protecting the HQ therefore protects more than one company.

The source describes wounds affecting future service and requiring a replacement for a subsequent mission; capture or death can permanently remove an officer. Exact chances and interruption durations are not supplied here. Keep the HQ behind a held line, check the path before an active ability, and avoid exposing it merely to chase a local advantage.

## How do you balance safe command with useful supply?

A safe headquarters still needs a workable logistical route. The Supply diary connects transfer rate to distance and terrain between depot and divisional HQ. Consider command safety and resupply together: moving an HQ forward can shorten a route but also put the officer closer to a breakthrough or unsupported flank.

Check the [supply guide](/guides/supply-and-logistics/) when the formation is consuming more than it receives. Use a protected route and appropriate depot placement as planning options; do not treat “as close as possible” as a universal positioning rule. The current battlefield and mission objective determine the tradeoff.
