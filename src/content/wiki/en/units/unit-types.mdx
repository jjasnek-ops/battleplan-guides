## Which unit roles does your formation need?

A useful formation needs troops for its main task and support for the obstacles it will face. Check reconnaissance, fortifications, mines, fire support and command separately. The official combined-arms diary describes nearby different unit types supporting each other, so a large collection of one type does not replace missing specialists.

| Unit or element | Documented role | Check before assigning it |
|---|---|---|
| Infantry | Hold territory and benefit from fortifications | Can it hold the approach or ground behind an advance? |
| Mechanized infantry | Mobile infantry support | Does the plan need to keep pace with armour? |
| Tanks | Mobile attacks and reserve response | Will the route and supply support continued movement? |
| Scouts | Reconnaissance and spotting | Have you identified what the attacking force will face? |
| Engineers | Clear mines, build stronger fortifications and assist assaults | Are mines or prepared positions blocking the task? |
| Artillery | Long-range barrages and support against fortifications | Does the formation need preparation before advancing? |
| Anti-tank guns | Support weapon element within mixed formations | Inspect the current unit card for its specific capabilities. |
| Anti-aircraft guns | Engage aircraft; the diary also describes ground engagement | Is air attack a relevant threat in this scenario? |
| Headquarters | Carry the officer and lead the formation | Is it protected and linked to a practical supply route? |

These are functional roles. No unverified damage, speed, range, cost or unlock table is presented. The [beginner checklist](/guides/beginner-guide/) explains how to check a deployable formation before the mission.

## How are companies, battalions and regiments organized?

The army hierarchy supports divisions, regiments, battalions and companies, with headquarters in the chain. The Army Constructor allows restructuring through connections between units. Regiments also matter to Regimental Plans, which allocate an operational area into zones. Inspect the hierarchy before changing a formation's structure.

## How do you choose what to add next?

Start with the task and name the missing capability before spending requisition. An attack through mines calls for engineering support; uncertain enemy positions call for reconnaissance; a formation intended to hold ground needs a defensible area. Check the mission's logistical limit after any change.

1. State the task: hold, attack, scout or support.
2. Inspect the units already attached.
3. Add or redistribute a specialist only when it addresses a concrete gap.
4. Recheck the officer, connections and total logistical weight.
5. Test the revised organization in the current tutorial or a manageable operation.

There is no verified universal “best division template” here. Use the [Army Constructor guide](/units/army-constructor/) to turn a missing role into a deployable formation, [officers and command](/units/officers-and-command/) to inspect its leadership, and the [combat guide](/guides/combat-guide/) to connect specialists to battlefield tasks.
